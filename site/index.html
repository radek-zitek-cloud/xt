Coordination is xt's job.
The work is the agents'.

An agent only needs a shell and a prompt. xt owns everything around it: who talks to whom, what is open, what it costs, and what needs you.

Organize

A hierarchy, not a mesh. You talk to one agent; it talks to the lead; the lead runs the team.

  • Liaison turns what you want into goals, reads them back to you and asks you only real decisions.
  • Lead designs the team the work needs, writes roles and skills, and asks to hire.
  • Members each run in their own terminal and report up. xt send refuses anything outside the chain.
Protocol Architecture

Decide

The team runs without you watching. You are needed only for decisions.

  • Approvals: every hire and every schedule waits for your yes.
  • One Inbox for questions, approvals and alerts, in the TUI or xt inbox, with a desktop notification, and exactly one notification when a goal you gave is done.
  • Questions that say what answer they take: yes/no, two to four options with their consequences and a recommendation, or open; xt checks your answer against it (your own words where the question allows them), hires and schedules are yes/no questions too, and the TUI keeps your answers in view for a week.
  • Standing rules instead of step-by-step instructions: "if I don't answer within the hour, pick the top story yourself, at most six a day".
  • Corrections become rules: a correction from you is recorded as a lesson, and the rule that allowed the mistake is fixed.

Verify

Independent quality checks, release candidates, acceptance against the spec.

  • A quality analyst agent checks every delivery criterion by criterion, in a clean copy of the code.
  • Candidates first: a release starts as -rc1; production stays on the accepted version until every card passes.
  • Evidence from the ledger: "delivered exactly once" is proven from the records, not taken on trust.

Measure & bound

Know what each agent holds and costs. Keep it inside the lines.

  • Context per agent (~211k/258k, yellow at 70%, red at 85%), tokens and estimated cost per turn and per goal, and the subscription allowance: Codex's week, Claude's five-hour and weekly windows.
  • No desktop or browser control for agents, and none of your account connectors (mail, files, calendar) unless you opt one in. Claude Code agents get a checked permissions file each.
  • Upgrades you can undo: xt version use switches to a release after checks and a verified snapshot; xt version rollback goes back.
  • Survives restarts: xt down and xt restart --all bring back the team that was running; agents recover from files and a brief, not from memory. An agent resumed outside xt, without its settings (say, by the terminal multiplexer after a power cycle), is flagged with xt restart as the fix.

Chat with the team

The everyday way to work with xt: one conversation with the liaison, in your terminal.

  • Narrative first: the liaison tells you what happened in plain words; when it needs you, the message ends in a question.
  • Every question says what answer it takes: yes or no; two to four options, each with its consequence and one recommended, sometimes with room for your own words; or open.
  • Approvals are questions too: a hire, a schedule or a goal ready to dispatch is a yes/no question, answered in the same place.
  • Answered once, by you: tab picks the waiting question; xt checks the answer against its type and accepts it only from your own terminal.

The whole team on one screen

When you want to see how the work flows, or why it stopped: xt tui. Three bands: Team and the Inbox on top, Work or Flow in the middle, Detail at the bottom. What needs you comes first, every pane is titled by its key, the newest is on top everywhere, and a key line shows what the focused pane can do.

Inbox

Three groups

Needs you (questions, approvals, alerts, until you act), Notifications (done goals and reports since you last looked) and unread Friction. Your answers and what you've seen stay a week, folded: space opens them.

Team

By harness

One column: each harness's account windows as bars with their reset time, then its agents: a dot in the state's colour (a red ! for one running without xt's launch settings), model and context used. Select the header or a harness line for versions, reading ages and why a window is unknown.

Work

An outline

Goals with their tasks under them, open, done or failed, and how many are done; fold them, or show open work only.

Flow

A lane chart

One lane per agent and one arrow per message, newest on top, marked by its type; focus on one agent or one goal, or filter by text. It shares the middle pane with Work: 2 and 3 switch.

Detail

The whole thread

The selected item with its goal, tasks and every reply in time order, and where you are in it.

Supervisor

A pop-up on v

What the supervisor did, newest first. A failed wake-up or notification now raises an Inbox alert.

Honest limits

xt is 0.x and about a week old. This is what it doesn't do yet.

  • Tested on Linux only, with Herdr. Daily use so far is one team, mostly on Claude Code, with one Codex and one pi agent; other setups have seen little real use.
  • The hierarchy is enforced by xt, not by a sandbox. A determined agent could still reach around it through its shell.
  • Costs are estimates from public list prices, not bills. No budgets yet.
  • Command-line tools that hold your credentials are ordinary programs to a harness; xt can't switch them off. A Claude agent's permissions file can refuse them; a Codex agent is bounded only by its sandbox's review.
