Coordination is xt's job.
The work is the agents'.

An agent only needs a shell and a prompt. xt owns everything around it: who talks to whom, what is open, what it costs, and what needs you.

Organize

A hierarchy, not a mesh. You talk to one agent; it talks to the lead; the lead runs the team.

  • Liaison turns what you want into goals, reads them back to you and asks you only real decisions.
  • Lead designs the team the work needs, writes roles and skills, and asks to hire.
  • Members each run in their own terminal and report up. xt send refuses anything outside the chain.
Protocol Architecture

Decide

The team runs without you watching. You are needed only for decisions.

  • Approvals: every hire and every schedule waits for your yes.
  • One Inbox for questions, approvals and alerts, in the TUI or xt inbox, with a desktop notification, and exactly one notification when a goal you gave is done.
  • Decisions with options: each question offers two or three options with their consequences and a recommendation; you answer with a number or your own words, and the TUI keeps your answers in view for a week.
  • Standing rules instead of step-by-step instructions: "if I don't answer within the hour, pick the top story yourself, at most six a day".
  • Corrections become rules: a correction from you is recorded as a lesson, and the rule that allowed the mistake is fixed.

Verify

Independent quality checks, release candidates, acceptance against the spec.

  • A quality analyst agent checks every delivery criterion by criterion, in a clean copy of the code.
  • Candidates first: a release starts as -rc1; production stays on the accepted version until every card passes.
  • Evidence from the ledger: "delivered exactly once" is proven from the records, not taken on trust.

Measure & bound

Know what each agent holds and costs. Keep it inside the lines.

  • Context per agent (~211k/258k, yellow at 70%, red at 85%), tokens and estimated cost per turn and per goal, and the subscription allowance: Codex's week, Claude's five-hour and weekly windows.
  • No desktop or browser control for agents, and none of your account connectors (mail, files, calendar) unless you opt one in. Claude Code agents get a checked permissions file each.
  • Upgrades you can undo: xt version use switches to a release after checks and a verified snapshot; xt version rollback goes back.
  • Survives restarts: xt down and xt restart --all bring back the team that was running; agents recover from files and a brief, not from memory.

Inbox

Three groups

Needs you (questions, approvals, alerts, until you act), New (done goals and reports since you last looked) and unread Friction. Your answers and what you've seen stay a week, folded: space opens them.

Team

By harness

Each harness's account windows as bars with their reset time, then its agents: a dot in the state's colour, model and context used.

Work

An outline

Goals with their tasks under them, open, done or failed, and how many are done; fold them, or show open work only.

Flow

A lane chart

One lane per agent and one arrow per message, marked by its type; filter to an agent, a goal or some text. Scroll it with the wheel.

Detail

The whole thread

The selected item with its goal, tasks and every reply in time order, and where you are in it.

Supervisor

A pop-up on v

What the supervisor did, newest first. A failed wake-up or notification now raises an Inbox alert.

What xt can do

Each of these has been shown in real runs, not only in tests.

01

Persistent, named agents

A liaison, a lead and members, each in its own workspace, working while you're away.

02

You decide, they deliver

Questions wait in one Inbox with a notification. You never have to watch a pane.

03

A supervisor and a ledger

Every message is delivered and logged. Nothing gets lost, and every step can be audited.

04

Goals, read back first

The liaison reads a goal back to you, lets you revise it, and dispatches it exactly once.

05

Schedules

Daily wakes at a set local time, hourly scouts, quiet hours that hold to the second.

06

Autonomy within rules

A newsroom picked and published stories when you didn't answer, and stopped at its daily limit.

07

Corrections become rules

Recorded lessons and a regular rules review keep the team's rules consistent and short.

08

A product team for xt itself

A PM and a quality analyst run xt's backlog, specs and releases in public. Only you pass the gates.

09

Independent acceptance

Every card is checked against its spec. A final PASS closes it; anything else waits for you.

10

Context and cost you can see

Per agent, per turn, per goal, and the week's subscription allowance.

11

Restarts without loss

The team that was running comes back, and each agent picks up from its brief.

12

Agents in bounds

No desktop, browser or account connectors by default, and no posing as the human.

Each checked against its spec by the product team's quality analyst before its final release; some on staging teams rather than in daily use yet.

16

Numbered options, each with its consequence, and a recommendation; answer with a number.

The five-hour and weekly windows in status, through a shipped status-line script.

Honest limits

xt is 0.x and a few days old. This is what it doesn't do yet.

  • Tested on Linux only, with Herdr. Most runs used Codex for every agent; the Claude Code and pi adapters have seen little real use.
  • The hierarchy is enforced by xt, not by a sandbox. A determined agent could still reach around it through its shell.
  • Costs are estimates from public list prices, not bills. No budgets yet.
  • Command-line tools that hold your credentials are ordinary programs to a harness; xt can't switch them off. A Claude agent's permissions file can refuse them; a Codex agent is bounded only by its sandbox's review.
xt is for coordinating accountable AI agent teams across harnesses, not for replacing the tools those agents use to do their work.
